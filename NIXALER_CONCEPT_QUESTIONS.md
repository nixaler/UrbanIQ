# NiXaler.Co — 50 Questions to Conceptualize the Business

Before the landing page copy becomes a real company, these are the questions that actually decide what NiXaler.Co *is*. Grouped by the ten areas that shape an entertainment booking & management agency.

---

## 1. Brand & Positioning
1. Is NiXaler.Co a talent agency (represents entertainers exclusively), a booking marketplace (connects both sides), or a full management company (career + booking + logistics)? The legal and financial model differs sharply between these.
2. What's the niche wedge — nightlife/DJs, weddings, corporate/brand activations, or all of the above from day one?
3. What's the geographic scope at launch — one metro area, a region, or national from the start?
4. What's the actual meaning/story behind the name "NiXaler," and does it need a tagline that explains the category (most people won't guess "entertainment booking" from the name alone)?
5. Who is the primary buyer of the brand promise — the client booking talent, or the entertainer looking for representation? The homepage, tone, and imagery change depending on which side you're designing for first.

## 2. Business Model & Revenue
6. Commission-based (a % cut of the entertainer's fee), markup-based (client pays more than the entertainer receives), flat management fee, or a hybrid?
7. What's the actual commission/markup percentage, and is it disclosed to both sides or only one?
8. Is there a membership or subscription tier for entertainers (pay to be on the roster) in addition to per-gig commission?
9. Does NiXaler.Co ever take exclusive representation of an act (they can't book elsewhere), or is it always non-exclusive?
10. What's the minimum viable deal size — is a $150 solo acoustic gig worth the same operational overhead as a $15,000 festival booking?

## 3. Talent Roster & Vetting
11. What's the actual vetting process — auditions, video submissions, reference checks, background checks, insurance verification?
12. Who decides who gets onto the roster, and is there a formal rejection/appeals process?
13. How many entertainers can realistically be actively managed per staff member before service quality drops?
14. What happens to underperforming or complaint-generating talent — probation, removal, mediation?
15. Does the roster include exclusive/proprietary acts (created and branded by NiXaler.Co itself) versus independent entertainers who sign on?

## 4. Client / Demand Side
16. Who is the target client at launch: individual consumers (weddings, private parties), B2B event planners, corporate marketing departments, or venues/clubs booking recurring talent?
17. Is there a minimum event size or budget NiXaler.Co will take on, and how is that communicated without turning away good early customers?
18. How much lead time is actually realistic for matching and confirming talent — and what happens for rush/last-minute requests?
19. What's the process when a client wants to negotiate the quoted price directly with the entertainer, bypassing the agency?
20. How are recurring clients (a hotel or club booking talent weekly) handled differently from one-off clients (a couple booking one wedding)?

## 5. Booking Operations & Workflow
21. What's the actual step-by-step flow from "client submits a request" to "entertainer is on stage" — who owns each handoff?
22. Who confirms availability — does the agency hold a live calendar per entertainer, or does it depend on entertainers responding to manual outreach?
23. What's the backup/replacement protocol if a booked act cancels close to the event date?
24. Who handles day-of logistics — load-in times, parking, green room needs, tech rider fulfillment — the agency, the venue, or the entertainer directly?
25. Is there a formal post-event feedback loop (from both client and entertainer) that feeds back into future matching and quality control?

## 6. Contracts, Legal & Risk
26. What business entity structure is this — LLC, S-corp — and in which state/jurisdiction, given that entertainment booking touches multiple states' talent-agent licensing laws?
27. Do any target states require a licensed talent agent or employment agency registration to legally book performers for a fee (many U.S. states do)?
28. What does the standard contract look like — is NiXaler.Co a party to the contract (agency-of-record model) or just a broker connecting two parties who sign directly?
29. What insurance does the company need — general liability, errors & omissions, and does it require entertainers to carry their own liability coverage?
30. Who is legally liable if a performer is injured, causes property damage, engages in misconduct, or a client cancels after signing?

## 7. Payments & Finance
31. Who collects money from the client — NiXaler.Co (and then pays out the entertainer) or does money flow client-to-entertainer with the agency invoicing separately?
32. What's the deposit structure (percentage, timing) and cancellation/refund policy on both the client side and entertainer side?
33. What's the payout timeline to entertainers after a completed gig — same day, net-15, net-30?
34. What happens when a client doesn't pay, or pays late — does the entertainer still get paid on schedule, with the agency absorbing the collection risk?
35. What payment processor/infrastructure is needed to handle deposits, split payouts, and possibly sales tax across jurisdictions?

## 8. Technology & Platform
36. Is version one a marketing website with manual, human-run matching (email/phone), or does it need a real booking platform (calendars, contracts, payments) from day one?
37. Does the roster need public profile pages (photos, reels, rates) or does that create disintermediation risk (clients booking the act directly next time)?
38. Is there a CRM tracking every entertainer's availability, rate history, and client feedback — and who owns/maintains it?
39. Should reviews/ratings be public-facing, or internal-only to protect entertainers from public criticism while still informing agency decisions?
40. What's the minimum tech needed to launch credibly versus what can be manual/spreadsheet-based for the first 20-50 bookings?

## 9. Marketing & Growth
41. How does NiXaler.Co get its first 10 entertainers and first 10 clients — direct outreach, industry relationships, paid ads, referral incentives?
42. Is growth client-led (attract event planners, then recruit talent to fill demand) or talent-led (build a roster first, then sell it to clients)?
43. What's the referral/word-of-mouth engine — do satisfied clients or entertainers get an incentive to bring in the next one?
44. Does content marketing (behind-the-scenes reels of gigs, entertainer spotlights) drive both client trust and entertainer recruitment simultaneously?
45. What's the competitive landscape — direct-booking platforms (GigSalad, The Bash), local competitors, and word-of-mouth agents — and what's the actual differentiator against them?

## 10. Team, Scaling & Long-Term
46. Who runs day-to-day matching and client relations in month one — is it a single founder wearing every hat, or is there already a small ops team?
47. At what booking volume does the model require a dedicated booking coordinator, contracts person, or finance person?
48. Does the company ever expand into adjacent services — event planning, venue partnerships, equipment rental, talent management beyond booking (PR, branding for the entertainers themselves)?
49. What does success look like at year one (bookings/month, revenue, roster size) versus the long-term vision (regional agency vs. national platform vs. acquisition target)?
50. What's the exit or long-term ownership plan — lifestyle business, venture-backed platform play, or acquisition by a larger event/talent company?
